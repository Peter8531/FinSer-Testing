# Superannuation Strategy

description: Produce an adviser working paper (paraplanning brief) for superannuation strategy optimisation covering contributions, investment strategy, and transition planning. Covers concessional and non-concessional contributions, carry-forward rules, salary sacrifice, spouse contributions, TTR strategies, pension phase transition, transfer balance cap management, and SMSF considerations. Output goes to paraplanning for formal SOA/ROA drafting. Triggers on "super strategy", "superannuation", "salary sacrifice", "super contributions", "transition to retirement", "TTR", "pension phase", "transfer balance cap", "downsizer contribution", or "SMSF".

## Workflow

### Step 1: Current Super Position

Gather for each household member:
- **Super fund(s)**: Fund name, balance, investment option, fees (admin + investment + insurance)
- **Insurance inside super**: Life cover amount, TPD cover amount and definition (any/own occupation), income protection (benefit amount, waiting period, benefit period)
- **Contribution history**: Concessional and non-concessional contributions this FY and prior years
- **Unused concessional cap carry-forward**: Available if total super balance was <$500,000 at prior 30 June (up to 5 years)
- **Total super balance** (as at prior 30 June): Determines eligibility for various strategies
- **Preservation age**: 60 (for those born after 1 July 1964)
- **Employment status**: Employee, self-employed, or retired
- **Employer SG rate**: Currently 11.5% (rising to 12% from 1 July 2025)

### Step 2: Contribution Optimisation

#### Concessional Contributions (taxed at 15% in the fund)

| Source | Current | Cap | Available Space |
|--------|---------|-----|----------------|
| Employer SG (11.5%) | | | |
| Salary sacrifice | | | |
| Personal deductible contributions | | | |
| **Total concessional** | | **$30,000** | |
| Carry-forward (prior unused caps) | | | |
| **Total available concessional space** | | | |

**Decision framework:**
- If marginal tax rate >15%: salary sacrifice is beneficial (tax saving = marginal rate minus 15%)
- If marginal rate is 16%: minimal benefit after contribution tax — may not justify reduced take-home pay
- If income + concessional contributions >$250,000: Division 293 applies (additional 15% = 30% total on contributions) — still beneficial if marginal rate is 45%
- Self-employed: personal deductible contributions (must lodge notice of intent with fund before lodging tax return or rolling over)

#### Non-Concessional Contributions (no tax on entry — already after-tax money)

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Total super balance (prior 30 June) | |
| Annual NCC cap | $120,000 |
| Bring-forward available? | Yes / No (balance must be <$1.9M) |
| Bring-forward amount | Up to $360,000 over 3 years |

**Decision framework:**
- If total super balance >$1.9M: **no non-concessional contributions allowed**
- If balance $1.78M-$1.9M: limited bring-forward ($240,000 over 2 years)
- Consider non-concessional contributions when: inheritance received, property sale, bonus, or redundancy
- Alternative: contribute to spouse's super if their balance is lower

#### Other Contribution Strategies

- **Spouse contributions**: Contribute up to $3,000 to low-income spouse's super for a tax offset of up to $540 (spouse income must be <$40,000)
- **Co-contributions**: Government matches 50c per $1 of personal NCC (up to $500) for incomes <$43,445 (reduced above $28,710)
- **Downsizer contributions**: Up to $300,000 per person from sale of home owned for 10+ years, age 55+, no cap test, does not count toward NCC cap. But: counted for Age Pension assets test from day one
- **First Home Super Saver (FHSS)**: Voluntary contributions (up to $15,000/year, $50,000 total) can be withdrawn for first home deposit — concessional contributions withdrawn at marginal rate minus 30% offset

**Considerations:**
- Life stage: younger clients can tolerate higher growth allocations; pre-retirees may de-risk gradually
- Fees: compare total cost across funds — even 0.5% p.a. difference compounds significantly over 20+ years
- Insurance: is the cover adequate? Is it eroding the balance? Would outside-super insurance be more cost-effective or provide better definitions (own occupation TPD)?
- SMSF: is the balance large enough to justify the costs ($500K+ typically)? Is the investment strategy document current? Annual audit compliance?

### Step 4: Transition to Retirement (TTR)

If the client has reached preservation age (60) but has not yet retired:

**TTR Strategy:**
1. Start a TTR income stream (account-based pension with 10% max drawdown)
2. Salary sacrifice an equivalent or greater amount into super
3. Net effect: boost super balance through concessional contributions while maintaining take-home pay via TTR income

### Step 7: Output — SOA Summary Request Input

This skill produces the **analytical working papers** that feed into the SOA Summary Request (assembled by the `strategy-request` skill). See [CONVENTIONS.md](../../CONVENTIONS.md) for house style rules.

**Feeds into Summary of Advice:**
- Super strategy summary (contribution plan, investment recommendation, insurance review)
- Contribution schedule for the financial year
- Fund consolidation recommendation (if applicable)
- Action items with responsible party and dates, each as a short directive sentence

**Feeds into Projection Parameters:**
- Contribution optimisation tables (concessional cap usage, carry-forward, NCC cap and bring-forward)
- TTR modelling (salary sacrifice vs TTR income stream, net benefit per year, projected additional super at retirement)
- Pension phase transition plan (transfer balance cap tracking, minimum drawdown schedule)
- Fund comparison (fees, insurance, performance) and consolidation analysis
- Source-of-Truth table for each input (document name, date, section)

**Feeds into Alternatives:**
- Strategies considered and dismissed with rationale (e.g., alternative contribution approaches, different fund structures, TTR vs direct salary sacrifice)

**Feeds into Product Considerations:**
- Super fund and platform comparisons (fees, insurance, investment options)
- Products or platforms dismissed with reasoning

**Feeds into Notes to AA or Paraplanning:**
- Gap items to request from client
- Risk profile, employment status, preservation age
- Key dates: contribution deadlines (30 June), BDBN expiry, insurance review, OFA renewal
- Flag any areas requiring further research or compliance review (e.g., defined benefit components, SMSF compliance)

**Calculation Workings (appendix):**
- Decision frameworks applied (marginal rate vs 15% contributions tax, Division 293 impact, NCC eligibility)
- Rationale linking each recommendation to client goals, risk profile, and tax position
- Getting started plan: pension phase transition timeline, rollover process, employer SG redirection

## Important Notes

- **This output is an adviser working paper. It is not a client-facing document.** The `strategy-request` skill assembles the final SOA Summary Request for paraplanning.
- All tables must follow house style: no bullets in cells, no em dashes, short directive sentences.
- Contribution caps reset each 1 July. Plan contributions before 30 June each year.
- Carry-forward unused concessional cap is only available if total super balance was <$500,000 at the prior 30 June.
- Non-concessional contributions are blocked entirely if total super balance is $1.9M or above.
- Division 293 applies to income + concessional contributions >$250,000. Still beneficial vs 45% marginal rate but the saving is reduced.
- TTR earnings are taxed at 15% (not tax-free). Only becomes tax-free when a full condition of release is met.
- Downsizer contributions are counted in the Centrelink assets test. Model the Age Pension impact before contributing.
- SMSF members are trustees and personally liable for compliance. Ensure investment strategy, audit, and sole purpose test are current.
- Insurance inside super erodes the balance. Review regularly to ensure the cover is still needed and cost-effective.
- The adviser and paraplanner ensure the final SOA/ROA meets Best Interest Duty (s961B) and FASEA Code of Ethics.
- Personal deductible contributions: must lodge a notice of intent with the fund and receive acknowledgement BEFORE lodging the tax return or rolling over.
