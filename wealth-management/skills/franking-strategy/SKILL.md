# Franking Credit Strategy

description: Produce an adviser working paper (paraplanning brief) for optimising portfolio construction and income planning around Australia's dividend imputation system. Covers franking credit calculation, grossed-up yields, portfolio tilts for income investors, and refund strategies for low-tax and pension phase investors. Output goes to paraplanning for formal SOA/ROA drafting. Triggers on "franking credits", "dividend imputation", "franked dividends", "grossed-up yield", "imputation credits", or "franking strategy".

## Workflow

### Step 1: Understand the Client's Tax Position

Franking credit value depends entirely on the investor's marginal tax rate:

| Tax Situation | Marginal Rate | Franking Credit Impact | Strategy Implication |
|---------------|-------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Super pension phase | 0% | Full refund of all franking credits | Maximum benefit — tilt portfolio toward high-franking stocks |
| Tax-free threshold (<$18,200) | 0% | Full refund | Same as pension phase |
| Low income ($18,201-$45,000) | 16% | Partial refund (company tax rate exceeds personal rate) | Strong benefit |
| Middle income ($45,001-$135,000) | 30% | Neutral for companies taxed at 30%, slight benefit for base rate entities (25%) | Moderate benefit |
| High income ($135,001-$190,000) | 37% | Top-up tax payable on franked dividends | Still reduces overall tax — but less valuable than for low-rate taxpayers |
| Top bracket (>$190,000) | 45% (+2% Medicare) | Top-up tax payable | Franking credits reduce tax but don't eliminate it |

**Franking credit calculation:**
- Franking credit = Cash dividend × (Company tax rate ÷ (1 - Company tax rate))
- For a company taxed at 30%: Franking credit = Cash dividend × (30/70) = Cash dividend × 0.4286
- For a base rate entity taxed at 25%: Franking credit = Cash dividend × (25/75) = Cash dividend × 0.3333
- Grossed-up dividend = Cash dividend + Franking credit
- Grossed-up yield = Grossed-up dividend ÷ Share price

### Step 3: Holding Period Rule Check

To be eligible for franking credits, shares must be held **"at risk"** for at least **45 days** (90 days for preference shares) during the qualification period (45 days before to 45 days after the ex-dividend date):

- Exclude the day of acquisition and the day of disposal
- "At risk" means no hedging or risk-reducing arrangements (e.g., put options, short selling the same stock)
- **Small shareholder exemption**: If total franking credits for the financial year are ≤$5,000, the holding period rule does not apply

### Step 4: Portfolio Optimisation

#### For Pension Phase / Zero-Tax Investors

These investors receive **full cash refunds** of all franking credits. Strategy:
- Tilt Australian equity allocation toward companies that pay **fully franked dividends** at the 30% corporate tax rate
- High-franking sectors: Major banks (CBA, WBC, NAB, ANZ), diversified miners (BHP, RIO), Telstra, Wesfarmers, Woolworths
- Consider LICs (Listed Investment Companies) like AFIC, Argo, Milton — they can smooth dividends and typically pay fully franked dividends from profit reserves
- **Total return comparison**: A 4% cash yield with full franking at 30% = 5.7% grossed-up yield. With full refund, the after-tax return is 5.7% — compare this to unfranked alternatives

#### For Accumulation Phase Super (15% tax)

- Franking credits at 30% offset 15% tax plus provide a 15% refund
- Effective tax on fully franked dividends: **negative** (the fund receives a refund)
- Strong incentive to hold high-franking Australian equities inside super

#### For Personal Investors (Marginal Rates 30-47%)

- Franking credits reduce tax but don't eliminate it
- Still preferable to unfranked income (especially international dividends taxed at full marginal rate)
- Don't over-concentrate in Australian equities just for franking — diversification matters
- Consider: franked yield vs total return (growth + yield) across the whole portfolio

#### Vehicles Comparison

| Vehicle | Typical Franking | Other Considerations |
|---------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Direct ASX shares (Big 4 banks, BHP, etc.) | 100% franked | Concentration risk, no diversification |
| Australian equity ETFs (VAS, IOZ, A200) | Mostly franked (follows index) | Diversified, low cost, franking varies by year |
| LICs (AFIC, Argo, Milton) | 100% franked (typically) | Can smooth dividends, may trade at premium/discount to NTA |
| Australian equity managed funds | Varies | Higher fees, may be tax-inefficient (capital gains distributions) |
| International equities / ETFs | 0% franked | No franking, but access to global growth |
| A-REITs | Partially franked or unfranked | Income often includes tax-deferred component |
| Hybrids / preference shares | Varies | 90-day holding period rule applies |

**ATO refund timing**: Franking credit refunds are received after lodging the tax return (typically October-January for returns lodged on time). For pension phase super funds, refunds are received by the fund after lodging the fund's tax return. Plan cash flow accordingly — the refund is not received when the dividend is paid.

### Step 6: Risks and Considerations

- **Concentration risk**: Over-weighting Australian equities for franking can reduce diversification. Australia is ~2% of global market capitalisation — a reasonable AU allocation might be 20-40%, not 80%+
- **Sector concentration**: High-franking stocks are concentrated in financials and resources. This creates sector risk
- **Policy risk**: Franking credit refund policy has been politically contested. Changes to imputation or refund rules could reduce the benefit (especially for zero-tax entities)
- **Total return vs income**: A stock with lower franked yield but higher capital growth may deliver better total after-tax returns. Don't sacrifice total return for franking alone
- **Holding period rule**: Ensure shares are held "at risk" for 45 days to qualify for franking credits. Trading around ex-dividend dates purely for franking may fail this test
- **Capital losses cannot offset franking credits**: If a client has net capital losses, they cannot use them to reduce tax on franked dividends. Franking credits only offset tax on the grossed-up dividend

## Important Notes

- **This output is an adviser working paper for paraplanning — it is not a client-facing document.** Paraplanning drafts the formal SOA/ROA from this brief
- Franking credits are most valuable for investors paying 0% tax (pension phase super, low-income earners) — they receive cash refunds
- The 45-day holding period rule (90 days for preference shares) must be met for franking credit eligibility — ensure the client doesn't trade around ex-dividend dates
- Small shareholder exemption (≤$5,000 total franking credits per FY) removes the holding period rule for most retail investors
- Don't over-concentrate in Australian equities for franking — diversification and total return matter more than maximising franking
- Franking credit refunds are received after tax return lodgement, not when the dividend is paid — plan cash flow accordingly
- Base rate entities (company tax rate 25%) generate lower franking credits per dollar of dividend than companies taxed at 30%
- LICs can pay dividends from profit reserves in years when portfolio income is lower — this smoothing can be valuable for income-dependent retirees
- The adviser and paraplanner ensure the final SOA/ROA meets Best Interest Duty (s961B) and FASEA Code of Ethics
