# SMA Portfolio Mapping

description: Complete reference mapping for Akambo SMA portfolios. Covers the CA (Circumstances and Attitude) range for drawdown clients and the MA (Multi Asset) range for risk-profile-aligned clients. Includes portfolio specifications, asset allocation ranges, benchmark targets, client profile suitability, cross-range comparison, and strategy-to-SMA selection guidance including debt recycling. ## CA Range (Circumstances and Attitude)

The CA range is designed for clients in or approaching drawdown phase. Each portfolio targets a specific annual drawdown rate while managing the growth/defensive allocation to sustain that drawdown over the client's time horizon.

**Design principle:** The drawdown rate drives the asset allocation. Higher drawdown requires more defensive allocation to protect capital being drawn; lower drawdown allows more growth allocation to compound over time.

### CA Portfolio Specifications

| Portfolio | Annual Drawdown Target | Growth/Defensive Target | Benchmark (after fees) | Client Profile |
|-----------|----------------------|------------------------|----------------------|----------------|
| CA0 | 0% (nil drawdown) | 90/10 | CPI + 4.50% | No income draw. Long-term growth orientation within the CA framework. Suited to pre-retirees not yet drawing income or entities (e.g., trusts) reinvesting all returns |
| CA2 | 2% | 90/10 | CPI + 4.00% | Low income needs. Growth emphasis with minimal sustainable drawdown. Suited to early retirees with other income sources or partial drawdown requirements |
| CA4 | 4% | 70/30 | CPI + 3.50% | Low to moderate income. Growth emphasis with sustainable drawdown. Suited to retirees supplementing Age Pension or part-time income |
| CA6 | 6% | 70/30 | CPI + 3.00% | Moderate income. Growth bias with higher drawdown. Suited to retirees relying on portfolio for a meaningful portion of living expenses |
| CA8 | 8% | 50/50 | CPI + 2.50% | Moderate to high income. Balanced allocation with significant drawdown. Suited to retirees drawing heavily on portfolio with moderate time horizon |
| CA10 | 10% | 50/50 | CPI + 2.00% | Moderate to high income. Balanced allocation with high drawdown. Suited to retirees with shorter time horizon or high income requirement relative to balance |

### CA Range Patterns

| Pattern | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Growth/Defensive steps | 90/10 at 0-2% drawdown. 70/30 at 4-6% drawdown. 50/50 at 8-10% drawdown |
| Benchmark steps | CPI + 4.50% at CA0, decreasing 50bp per 2% drawdown increment |
| Allocation philosophy | Higher drawdown compresses the growth allocation because capital preservation becomes more important when the portfolio is being drawn down faster. The manager varies from target allocation to take advantage of opportunities |
| Tactical overlay | Actual allocation may drift from target. The investment committee adjusts positioning dynamically within the execution framework. For example, a 50/50 target portfolio may run at approximately 54/46 when conditions favour a slight growth tilt |
| Rebalancing | Managed by Akambo within the execution framework. Tax-aware rebalancing with consideration of CGT timing and franking credits |

### CA Selection Guide

| Client Situation | Recommended CA | Rationale |
|-----------------|---------------|-----------|
| Pre-retiree, 5+ years to retirement, no income need | CA0 | Maximum growth. No drawdown drag. Build balance before transition |
| Early retiree, Age Pension plus small top-up | CA2 | Minimal drawdown preserves growth orientation. Other income covers most expenses |
| Retiree, moderate expenses, part-time work or Pension supplement | CA4 | Sustainable 4% draw with growth bias. Standard retirement drawdown rate |
| Retiree, primary income source from portfolio | CA6 | Higher draw rate with growth protection. Balances income need against longevity |
| Retiree, high expenses relative to balance, moderate horizon | CA8 | Significant drawdown requires balanced allocation. Monitor sustainability annually |
| Retiree, short horizon or high income requirement | CA10 | Maximum drawdown in range. Defensive tilt protects near-term income. Review annually for sustainability |

## MA Range (Multi Asset)

The MA range is designed for risk-profile-aligned portfolios. Each portfolio maps to a standard risk profile (A through E, defensive to aggressive) with defined asset class ranges and volatility characteristics.

**Design principle:** The risk profile drives the asset allocation. The manager operates within defined asset class ranges, adjusting tactically within those bands. No specific drawdown rate is targeted; the focus is total return appropriate to the risk profile.

### MA Portfolio Specifications

| Portfolio | Risk Profile | Growth/Income Target | Volatility | Minimum Timeframe |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|------------|-------------------|
| MA-A | A (Defensive) | 25/75 | Low | 3 years |
| MA-B | B (Conservative) | 45/55 | Low/Medium | 4 years |
| MA-C | C (Balanced) | 60/40 | Medium | 5 years |
| MA-D | D (Growth) | 75/25 | High | 6 years |
| MA-E | E (Aggressive) | 90/10 | Very High | 7 years |

### MA Asset Class Ranges

| Asset Class | MA-A | MA-B | MA-C | MA-D | MA-E |
|-------------|------|------|------|------|------|
| Cash | 16-36% | 10-30% | 0-20% | 0-15% | 0-13% |
| Fixed Interest | 30-69% | 20-60% | 20-50% | 10-35% | 0-17% |
| Australian Shares | 0-15% | 6-26% | 16-38% | 25-45% | 35-55% |
| International Shares | 0-15% | 4-24% | 14-34% | 25-45% | 35-55% |

### MA Range Patterns

| Pattern | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Defensive allocation (Cash + Fixed Interest) | Approximately 85% midpoint at MA-A, decreasing to approximately 15% at MA-E |
| Equity allocation (Aust + Int'l Shares) | Approximately 15% at MA-A, increasing to approximately 85% at MA-E |
| Range width | Wide bands give the manager significant tactical flexibility within each profile |
| Tactical overlay | Manager adjusts within the defined ranges based on market conditions and outlook. This is a key differentiator from static index approaches |
| Minimum timeframe | Increases with equity allocation. Reflects the longer recovery period needed for higher-volatility portfolios |

### MA Selection Guide

| Client Situation | Recommended MA | Rationale |
|-----------------|---------------|-----------|
| Capital preservation, short horizon, low risk tolerance | MA-A | Maximum defensive allocation. Low volatility. Suited to clients who cannot afford a significant drawdown |
| Conservative investor, some growth, 4+ year horizon | MA-B | Moderate income bias with growth component. Suited to conservative accumulators or conservative debt recycling |
| Balanced investor, medium horizon, comfortable with volatility | MA-C | Classic balanced allocation. Suited to mid-career accumulators or balanced debt recycling |
| Growth investor, long horizon, comfortable with higher volatility | MA-D | Growth emphasis with some defensive buffer. Suited to younger accumulators or aggressive debt recycling |
| Aggressive investor, 7+ year horizon, maximum growth | MA-E | Maximum equity allocation. Highest expected return and volatility. Suited to long-term accumulators with strong risk tolerance |

## Cross-Range Comparison (CA vs MA)

The CA and MA ranges serve different purposes. This table maps comparable growth allocations across both ranges.

| Growth Target | CA Equivalent | MA Equivalent | Key Difference |
|---------------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| 90/10 | CA0, CA2 | MA-E | CA targets nil/2% drawdown with growth. MA targets aggressive total return with no drawdown assumption |
| 75/25 | No direct match | MA-D | MA-D fills a growth slot that the CA range does not offer as a standalone target |
| 70/30 | CA4, CA6 | No direct match | CA targets 4-6% drawdown. Closest MA is MA-D (75/25) or MA-C (60/40) |
| 60/40 | No direct match | MA-C | MA-C fills a balanced slot that the CA range skips (CA steps from 70/30 to 50/50) |
| 50/50 | CA8, CA10 | No direct match | CA targets 8-10% drawdown. Closest MA is MA-B (45/55) |
| 45/55 | No direct match | MA-B | MA-B fills a conservative slot more defensive than any CA portfolio |
| 25/75 | No direct match | MA-A | MA-A is far more defensive than anything in the CA range |

### When to Use CA vs MA

| Criterion | Use CA | Use MA |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Client phase | In drawdown or approaching drawdown | Accumulation or no near-term drawdown |
| Primary objective | Sustainable income at a target drawdown rate | Total return aligned to risk profile |
| Income requirement | Specified drawdown rate (0-10% p.a.) | No specific drawdown target |
| Asset allocation driver | Drawdown rate determines growth/defensive split | Risk profile determines growth/income split |
| Typical client | Retiree, pre-retiree with income target, pension phase | Young professional, mid-career accumulator, trust reinvesting |
| Debt recycling | Use where recycled portfolio must produce a specific income rate to service the investment loan | Use where income production is secondary to growth and the loan is serviced from other cashflow |

## Debt Recycling: SMA Selection

For debt recycling strategies, the SMA must produce assessable income to support the interest deduction on the investment loan. SMA selection depends on whether the recycled portfolio needs to service the loan directly or whether the client has other cashflow to cover loan repayments.

### Income-Focused Debt Recycling (MA Range)

Where the recycled portfolio must produce income to service the investment loan, the MA range provides a clear income allocation. The income component (Cash + Fixed Interest + equity distributions) services the loan while the growth component builds the portfolio.

| Debt Recycling Stage | Suggested MA | Income/Growth | Rationale |
|---------------------|-------------|---------------|-----------|
| Early (high LVR, building equity) | MA-A or MA-B | 75/25 or 55/45 | Prioritise income certainty to cover loan repayments. Higher defensive allocation reduces risk of income shortfall |
| Mid (debt reducing, equity growing) | MA-B or MA-C | 55/45 or 40/60 | Can afford slightly more growth tilt as loan balance shrinks relative to portfolio value |
| Late (minimal debt, strategy winding down) | MA-C or MA-D | 40/60 or 25/75 | Transition toward growth as the income servicing obligation drops. Portfolio now large relative to remaining debt |

### Growth-Focused Debt Recycling (CA Range)

Where the client services the investment loan from salary or other cashflow (not from the recycled portfolio), the CA range can be used for its drawdown targeting. The portfolio produces some income (distributions) but the primary focus is capital growth or sustainable drawdown.

| Client Situation | Suggested CA | Rationale |
|-----------------|-------------|-----------|
| High income, loan fully serviced from salary | CA0 or CA2 | Maximum growth. Loan serviced externally. Portfolio income is a bonus, not a requirement |
| Moderate income, partial loan servicing from portfolio | CA4 | Moderate drawdown covers part of loan interest. Remaining serviced from salary |

### Debt Recycling SMA Selection Decision Tree

| Question | If Yes | If No |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Does the client need the recycled portfolio to produce income to service the investment loan? | Use MA range (income-focused) | Use CA range or MA-D/MA-E (growth-focused) |
| Is the client in early debt recycling with high LVR? | MA-A or MA-B for income certainty | Consider MA-C or MA-D for growth |
| Is the client's marginal tax rate 37%+ ? | Debt recycling is more tax-effective. Higher deduction value supports a slightly more aggressive portfolio | Still viable at 30%+ but monitor cost of carry closely |
| Does the client have a 7+ year time horizon? | Can tolerate more growth allocation (MA-C, MA-D) even in early stages | Stay conservative (MA-A, MA-B) to manage sequence risk |
| Is the client servicing the loan entirely from salary? | CA0 or CA2 for maximum growth. Income production is secondary | MA range to ensure income services the loan |

### Serviceability Check

Before selecting the SMA for debt recycling, confirm that the expected distribution yield of the chosen portfolio covers the after-tax interest cost of the investment loan:

| Check | Calculation |
|-------|------------|
| After-tax interest cost | Gross interest rate x (1 minus marginal tax rate) |
| Expected distribution yield | Based on the income allocation of the selected MA portfolio |
| Shortfall (if any) | After-tax interest cost minus expected distribution yield. Client must fund the shortfall from other cashflow |
| Serviceability confirmed | Distribution yield covers after-tax interest cost, or client has documented surplus cashflow to cover the shortfall |

Record this check in the SOA Summary Request under Projection Parameters. Confirm income production with Akambo for the selected SMA type.

## Risk Profile to SMA Mapping

Risk profile is determined per client. There are no default mappings. The tables below are guidance for the adviser when selecting an SMA based on risk profile assessment.

### Accumulation Phase (MA Range)

| Risk Profile Score | Typical MA | Notes |
|-------------------|-----------|-------|
| A (Defensive) | MA-A | Rare for accumulation. Confirm short time horizon or very low risk tolerance |
| B (Conservative) | MA-B | Conservative accumulator. Confirm timeframe supports growth objective |
| C (Balanced) | MA-C | Standard balanced accumulator. Most common for mid-career clients |
| D (Growth) | MA-D | Growth accumulator with long horizon. Confirm comfort with higher volatility |
| E (Aggressive) | MA-E | Maximum growth. 7+ year horizon. Confirm risk tolerance documentation |

### Drawdown Phase (CA Range)

| Client Need | Typical CA | Notes |
|------------|-----------|-------|
| No drawdown (pre-retirement accumulation within CA framework) | CA0 | Growth-oriented but within the drawdown-managed framework |
| Low drawdown (2% p.a.) | CA2 | Minimal income draw. Other income sources cover most expenses |
| Standard drawdown (4% p.a.) | CA4 | Most common starting point for new retirees |
| Moderate drawdown (6% p.a.) | CA6 | Higher income need. Monitor sustainability |
| High drawdown (8% p.a.) | CA8 | Significant income draw. Review annually |
| Maximum drawdown (10% p.a.) | CA10 | Highest drawdown in range. Short horizon or high income need. Review annually |

### Risk Profile Mismatch

If the risk profile score does not match the selected SMA, document the rationale in the SOA Summary Request. Common reasons for mismatch:

| Scenario | Example | Rationale |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Risk score higher than selected portfolio | Scored D (Growth) but selected CA4 (70/30) | Client is in drawdown phase. Drawdown requirement constrains growth allocation. Risk tolerance supports the allocation but drawdown sustainability takes priority |
| Risk score lower than selected portfolio | Scored B (Conservative) but selected MA-C (60/40) | Long time horizon (15+ years) supports higher growth allocation. Client understands and accepts higher short-term volatility for long-term objective. Documented acceptance |
| Debt recycling override | Scored D (Growth) but selected MA-B (45/55) | Income-focused debt recycling requires higher defensive allocation to service loan. Growth allocation will increase as debt reduces (staged transition plan documented) |

## Important Notes

- **This is a reference skill for the adviser and paraplanning team. It is not a client-facing document.**
- SMA selection must be tailored to client circumstances. These mappings are guidance, not prescriptive rules.
- Risk profile is determined per client based on their circumstances, risk tolerance, and investment timeframe. Where different entities have different risk profiles, specify the mapping explicitly per entity.
- All investment is delivered through the First Financial Execution Framework via Akambo SMA. See the `strategic-library` skill for the full framework documentation.
- The SMA may hold direct assets, ETFs, or managed funds as building blocks. That is Akambo's implementation decision within the framework.
- Actual allocations may vary from target. The investment committee adjusts positioning dynamically within the execution framework.
- Debt recycling SMA selection must ensure the portfolio produces assessable income to support the interest deduction. See the `debt-recycling` skill for full strategy documentation.
- The adviser and paraplanner ensure the final SOA/ROA meets Best Interest Duty (s961B) and FASEA Code of Ethics.
