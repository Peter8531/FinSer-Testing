## Alternative Strategies (Considered and Dismissed)

| | Alternative (Heading + Explanation + Dismissal) |
|---|------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Transition to Retirement (TTR) pension. The client has reached preservation age (60) and could commence a TTR pension to draw tax-free income (over 60) while continuing to work. The TTR income could accelerate the offset rebuild and mortgage payoff by approximately 8 months. However, the client's concessional contribution cap is already consumed by employer SG ($26,400 of $30,000). The classic TTR benefit depends on salary sacrificing additional concessional contributions to replace the pension drawdown at a tax advantage (15% in vs 0% out). Without meaningful CC cap headroom, TTR simply depletes super earlier for a 4.00% guaranteed return (offset interest saved) versus an expected 5.95% after-tax return inside super. The acceleration benefit does not justify the lower compounding on the drawn funds. Dismissed. |
| 2 | Debt recycling. Converting non-deductible mortgage debt to deductible investment debt via a split loan and recycled investment portfolio is a viable wealth-building strategy. However, debt recycling requires a minimum 10-year time horizon to manage sequence risk and allow the investment portfolio to compound through market cycles. The client is 62 with a 3 to 5 year retirement horizon. The timeframe is too short to justify the complexity, establishment costs, and market risk. Maximising super contributions in the remaining working years is more appropriate. Dismissed. |
| 3 | Non-super investing (personal investment portfolio). The client could invest surplus cashflow outside super for flexibility and no preservation restrictions. However, at a 39% marginal tax rate (including Medicare levy), earnings outside super are taxed significantly more than inside super (15% on earnings in accumulation, 0% in pension phase after meeting a condition of release). The client has already reached preservation age and can access super once retired. Flexibility benefit is minimal. Super is materially more tax-effective for retirement savings at this marginal rate. Dismissed. |

## Alternative Product Considerations

| | Alternative Product (Heading + Explanation + Dismissal) |
|---|-------------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Retain Australian Super. AustralianSuper offers low administration fees and strong default investment options. However, it operates as a pooled trust. The client holds units in a commingled pool with no control over which parcels are sold, CGT timing, or tax lot selection. Embedded capital gains are socialised across unitholders. Rebalancing occurs at fund level with no adviser visibility. Platform SMA via Akambo provides beneficial ownership in the client's name, full tax lot control, specialist-managed exposures within the First Financial execution framework, and real-time transparency. As the client approaches retirement and will transition to pension phase, beneficial ownership and CGT control become increasingly important. Dismissed in favour of platform rollover. |
| 2 | Index funds or passive ETFs (VAS, IOZ, VGS) as standalone holdings. Low cost and broadly diversified. However, not aligned with the First Financial execution framework. The framework delivers dynamic portfolio management with tactical overlay, quality-filtered asset selection, and continuous risk management via the SMA. Index funds provide static market exposure without active oversight, tactical adjustment, or drawdown targeting. The SMA may use ETFs as building blocks within the managed portfolio, but that is an implementation decision within the framework, not a client-level recommendation. Dismissed. |
