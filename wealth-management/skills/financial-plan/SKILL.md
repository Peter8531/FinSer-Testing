# Financial Plan

description: Build or update a comprehensive financial plan covering superannuation strategy, retirement projections, Age Pension modelling, education funding, estate planning, and cash flow analysis. Use for new client onboarding, annual plan reviews, or scenario modelling. Triggers on "financial plan", "retirement plan", "can I retire", "super strategy", "education funding", "estate plan", "cash flow analysis", or "plan update".

## Workflow

### Step 1: Client Profile

Gather or confirm:
- **Demographics**: Age, spouse/partner age, dependents, life expectancy assumptions
- **Employment**: Current income (salary + super guarantee), expected pay rises, target retirement age, preservation age eligibility
- **Superannuation**: All super accounts with balances, fund names, investment options, insurance held within super
- **Other Accounts**: Personal investments, family trust, company, insurance bonds, term deposits
- **SMSF**: If self-managed — current assets, investment strategy document, audit status
- **Income sources**: Salary, business income, rental income (including negative gearing), dividends (franked/unfranked), Centrelink benefits, super pension income
- **Expenses**: Current annual spending, expected changes (mortgage payoff, children's independence, aged care)
- **Liabilities**: Mortgage, HECS-HELP debt, investment loans, personal debt
- **Insurance**: Life (inside/outside super), TPD (any occupation/own occupation), income protection (indemnity value, benefit period), trauma/critical illness (outside super only), private health insurance
- **Estate**: Will, testamentary trusts, binding death benefit nominations (lapsing/non-lapsing), enduring power of attorney, enduring guardian (state-specific)

Key inputs:

- Australian tax brackets (0% to $18,200 / 16% to $45,000 / 30% to $135,000 / 37% to $190,000 / 45% above $190,000) plus 2% Medicare Levy
- Franking credit offsets and refunds

- Savings direction: concessional super contributions (salary sacrifice), non-concessional super contributions, personal investments, trust distributions

### Step 3: Superannuation Projections

**Contribution Strategy:**
- Super Guarantee: currently 11.5% of ordinary time earnings (rising to 12% from 1 July 2025)
- Concessional contribution cap: $30,000/year (includes SG + salary sacrifice + personal deductible contributions)
- Carry-forward unused concessional cap: available for up to 5 years if total super balance <$500,000
- Non-concessional contribution cap: $120,000/year (or $360,000 using 3-year bring-forward if balance <$1.9M)
- Spouse contributions (tax offset up to $540 for contributing spouse)
- Downsizer contributions: up to $300,000 per person from sale of family home (age 55+, no cap test)
- Division 293 tax: additional 15% on concessional contributions if income + contributions >$250,000
- Contributions tax: 15% on concessional contributions within the fund

**Accumulation Phase:**
- Current super balance(s)
- Projected contributions (SG + salary sacrifice + non-concessional)
- Expected return by investment option
- Tax on earnings: 15% (10% on capital gains held >12 months with 33% CGT discount)
- Monte Carlo simulation: probability of success at various spending levels

**Transition to Retirement (if applicable):**
- Available from preservation age (60 for those born after 1/7/1964)
- Maximum drawdown: 10% of account balance per year
- Earnings taxed at 15% (not tax-free like full pension)
- Strategy: salary sacrifice + TTR income stream to boost super while maintaining income

**Pension Phase:**
- Condition of release: preservation age + retired, or age 65
- Transfer Balance Cap (TBC): $1.9 million maximum transfer to pension phase
- Earnings in pension phase: **tax-free**
- Minimum pension drawdown rates:
  - Age 60-64: 4%
  - Age 65-74: 5%
  - Age 75-79: 6%
  - Age 80-84: 7%
  - Age 85-89: 9%
  - Age 90-94: 11%
  - Age 95+: 14%
- Pension income: tax-free for members aged 60+

### Step 4: Age Pension Modelling

Model Centrelink Age Pension eligibility (age 67):
- **Income test**: Pension reduces by 50c for each dollar of assessable income above the threshold (single: ~$204/fortnight, couple combined: ~$360/fortnight)
- **Assets test**: Pension reduces by $3.00 per $1,000 of assets above the threshold (homeowner single: ~$301,750, couple: ~$451,500)
- **Deeming rates**: Financial assets are deemed to earn income regardless of actual returns (first ~$60,400 at 0.25%, balance at 2.25% for singles)
- The more restrictive test (income or assets) determines the pension amount
- Maximum single rate: ~$1,144/fortnight; couple combined: ~$1,725/fortnight
- Superannuation in accumulation phase is **exempt** from the assets test until Age Pension age
- Account-based pensions are assessed under **both** income and assets tests

**Key Output:**
- Projected super balance at retirement
- Sustainable drawdown rate
- Age Pension entitlement (full, part, or nil) based on means testing
- Probability of not running out of money (target >85%)
- Optimal timing for super pension phase commencement

### Step 5: Goal-Specific Analysis

#### Education Funding
- Children's ages and target education start
- School fees (primary, secondary, tertiary)
- No tax-advantaged education savings vehicle in Australia (no 529 equivalent)
- Consider: education bonds (insurance bonds held >10 years — tax-paid within the bond), direct savings, family trust distributions
- HECS-HELP as alternative to upfront payment (indexed to CPI, repaid through tax system at income-contingent rates)
- Required savings to reach goal

#### Estate Planning
- No estate tax or inheritance tax in Australia (abolished 1979)
- No gift tax — but Centrelink has gifting rules (>$10,000/year or >$30,000 over 5 years is assessed for 5 years)
- **Super death benefits tax**: Taxable component paid to non-tax dependants (adult children) taxed at 15-30% + Medicare levy. Tax-free to tax dependants (spouse, children <18, financial dependants)
- **Binding Death Benefit Nomination (BDBN)**: Lapsing (expires every 3 years) vs non-lapsing — review and renew
- **Testamentary trusts**: Income distributed to minor beneficiaries taxed at adult marginal rates (significant income-splitting advantage)
- **Enduring Power of Attorney**: Financial — state-specific legislation
- **Enduring Guardian / Advance Health Directive**: Medical and lifestyle — state-specific
- Beneficiary review across all super funds and insurance policies

#### Risk Management
- **Life insurance**: Needs analysis (income replacement, debt payoff, education). Inside-super premiums are effectively pre-tax but erode the super balance. Outside-super premiums are not deductible for individuals (deductible for super funds)
- **TPD (Total and Permanent Disability)**: "Own occupation" definition can only be held outside super since April 2020. "Any occupation" available inside super
- **Income protection**: APRA reforms limit inside-super IP to indemnity value with maximum 2-year benefit period. Outside-super can provide agreed value, longer benefit periods (to age 65). Premiums outside super are tax-deductible
- **Trauma / critical illness**: Cannot be held inside super — must be held personally. Not tax-deductible
- **Private health insurance**: Review for Medicare Levy Surcharge avoidance (1-1.5% for singles >$93,000, families >$186,000 without appropriate cover)
- **Aged care planning**: Refundable Accommodation Deposit (RAD) vs Daily Accommodation Payment (DAP), means-tested care fees, impact on Age Pension

### Step 7: Recommendations

Prioritised action items:
1. Super contribution strategy (maximise concessional cap, consider non-concessional, carry-forward, spouse contributions)
2. Asset allocation adjustments (across super and personal investments)
3. Tax optimisation (salary sacrifice, franking credit strategies, CGT discount timing, negative gearing, asset location between super and personal)
4. Centrelink optimisation (structuring assets to maximise Age Pension entitlement)
5. Insurance structure review (inside vs outside super, cover adequacy, own occupation TPD)
6. Estate planning: BDBN review, will update, testamentary trust consideration
7. Aged care planning (if relevant)

- Action item checklist

## Important Notes

- Financial plans are living documents — review and update annually or after major life events
- Be conservative with return assumptions — overestimating returns gives false confidence
- Tax planning is as important as investment returns — model tax implications of every recommendation including franking credits, CGT discount, and super tax concessions
- Super contribution timing is a major lever — model maximising concessional caps, carry-forward, and non-concessional strategies
- Age Pension modelling is critical for most Australian retirees — even partial entitlement is valuable
- Always stress-test the plan — a plan that only works in the base case is not a good plan
- Compliance: ensure recommendations align with Best Interest Duty (s961B Corporations Act) and FASEA Code of Ethics
- All personal advice must be documented in a Statement of Advice (SOA) or Record of Advice (ROA)
- Financial year runs 1 July to 30 June — all tax modelling must use this period
